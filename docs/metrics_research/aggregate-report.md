# Literature Audit and Tuning Summary for the Social Navigation Weight Matrix

## Scope and bottom line

The uploaded specification currently defines a 16-feature, 4-axis prior matrix on a `0..3` evidence scale, then renormalizes each axis into weights; it also plans to replace that prior with learned coefficients after Cohort A. The same file proposes a “validated subscale mix” for the final subjective instrument. fileciteturn0file1L600-L770 fileciteturn0file1L805-L813

The literature audit supports the overall direction of that design, but not every cell equally. The strongest direct evidence is concentrated in **proxemics**, **approach geometry**, and **explicit intent communication**. By contrast, **smoothness**, **heading jerk**, **behavioral naturalness**, **hesitation/freezing**, and **comfort-aware efficiency** are either indirect HRI evidence, transfer evidence from movement science, or framework-level guidance rather than direct perception studies. That means the current prototype matrix is structurally usable as a prior, but several cells should be treated as **weak priors or flip-candidates**, and safety should be treated as a **higher-order constraint** rather than a fully compensable addend in a flat weighted sum. citeturn0search10turn1search4turn1search16turn1search14turn2search0turn3view2turn2search10turn2search7turn4search0turn27view0turn25view0turn26view0turn28view3

The earlier broad report had already reached a similar conclusion for classic performance metrics: it treated spacing, intrusion, speed, signaling, and approach geometry as the strongest reusable evidence, while success, completion time, and generic path efficiency remained weaker priors. The new audit reinforces that split rather than overturning it. fileciteturn0file0L159-L196

## Bibliographic audit

Every source family cited in `social-navigation-metrics.md` corresponds to a real, traceable paper, survey, or foundational reference. The scale papers are real and well established: the Godspeed paper exists as the 2009 International Journal of Social Robotics article by Bartneck, Kulić, Croft, and Zoghbi; RoSAS exists as the 2017 ACM/IEEE HRI paper by Carpinella, Wyman, Perez, and Stroessner; and the PSI paper exists as the 2020 ACM THRI article *Measuring the Perceived Social Intelligence of Robots*. citeturn0search0turn0search1turn17view1

The core social-navigation evidence papers also exist and are relevant: Dautenhahn’s 2006 seated-approach study, Woods’ 2006 approach-direction methodology paper, Pacchierotti’s hallway proxemics studies, Takayama and Pantofaru’s proxemics paper, Mumm and Mutlu’s HRI proxemics paper, Neggers et al.’s 2022 passing-distance paper, Watanabe et al.’s 2015 navigation-intention communication paper, Palinko et al.’s 2020 intention-indication paper, Yu et al.’s 2023 co-navigation visualization paper, Dragan et al.’s 2013 legibility paper, the Trautman crowd-navigation papers, and the major surveys by Ríos-Martínez, Kruse, Mavrogiannis, Francis, Gao and Huang, and later benchmarking reviews. citeturn0search10turn0search11turn1search4turn1search16turn1search14turn2search0turn3view2turn2search10turn2search7turn4search0turn7search5turn21view0turn14search0turn14search5turn13search2turn4search3turn27view0turn23view1turn23view2

## Evidence strength by metric family

### Proxemics and approach behavior

This is the strongest evidence family in the entire matrix. The literature repeatedly shows that spacing, passing distance, intrusion into personal space, visible/side approach direction, and comfortable approach geometry are central to comfort, safety, and social appropriateness. The direct studies differ in setup, but they all converge on the same qualitative pattern: people prefer robots that keep appropriate distance, avoid frontal intrusions, and approach from geometries that remain visible and legible. citeturn0search10turn0search11turn1search4turn1search16turn1search14turn2search0turn14search0turn14search5

For weight tuning, this means the current strong priors on `min_human_robot_distance`, `proxemic_intrusion_dose`, and `human_aware_approach` are well supported. If any cells deserve top-tier prior mass before fitting, these do. The evidence is especially strong for **Safety** and **Awareness**, and moderately supportive for **Impression** through comfort and unobtrusiveness. The literature is much less supportive of mapping these same features strongly into **Dexterity**, except insofar as competent robots should also be socially well-behaved. citeturn2search0turn27view0turn24search1turn29search1

A second important implication is functional form. The literature supports **thresholded or saturating** comfort effects much more than a naive “more distance is always better” rule. So the feature transform should be non-linear even if the final per-axis aggregator is linear. That is especially true for `min_human_robot_distance`, `proxemic_intrusion_dose`, and `speed_near_humans_p95`. citeturn2search0turn14search0turn27view0

### Signaling, acknowledgement, and legibility

The next strongest family is explicit communicative behavior: projected or gestural intent cues, readable yielding, clear acknowledgement, and legible motion. Watanabe’s wheelchair-intention paper, Palinko’s hallway intersection paper, Yu’s co-navigation visualization paper, and Dragan’s legibility work all support the same high-level point: humans respond better when a robot’s upcoming motion can be inferred early and with low ambiguity. citeturn3view2turn2search10turn2search7turn4search0turn16search0

For the matrix, this strongly supports high priors for `gesture_response_success`, `acknowledgement_clarity`, `bystander_ack`, and `legibility` on **Awareness**. It also supports moderate priors on **Safety**, because clearer intent reduces uncertainty and perceived risk. Evidence for **Impression** is real but weaker than for awareness; evidence for **Dexterity** is plausible but mostly secondary. Put differently: these features should primarily explain “the robot understood the social situation,” not “the robot was fast” or “the robot was physically skilled.” citeturn4search0turn3view2turn2search10turn2search7turn25view0

This is also the place where the uploaded matrix is most defensible as written. The current high awareness scores for signaling-oriented metrics are consistent with both the classic legibility literature and newer social-navigation work. fileciteturn0file1L679-L709 citeturn4search0turn16search0turn13search2

### Smoothness, naturalness, and control quality

This family should stay in the model, but with more humility. The movement-science papers on SPARC and gait variability are real and valuable, yet they are **transfer evidence**, not direct HRI perception evidence. The Schulz paper is the only directly relevant surfaced source tying velocity-profile differences to HRI ratings, and even there the full-scale Godspeed results were largely non-significant. citeturn5search1turn8search0turn11search1turn10view3turn5search0turn5search4

That means `sparc`, `heading_jerk`, and `behavioral_naturalness` should not be given the same evidentiary status as proxemics or intent signaling. They are appropriate as **mechanistic priors** for **Impression** and, secondarily, **Dexterity**. They are much weaker as strong priors for **Safety** or **Awareness** unless local data later proves otherwise. In practical terms, these are good **1** or cautious **2** cells, but rarely good **3** cells on literature alone. citeturn5search1turn11search1turn27view0turn23view1

The same caution applies to `heading_jerk`. The conceptual bridge from translational smoothness to rotational smoothness is reasonable, and the social-motion literature supports readable, committed trajectories, but the specific evidence base for “yaw jerk drives perceived safety” is still quite indirect. That cell should remain provisional. citeturn16search0turn13search25turn4search0turn11search1

### Success, efficiency, hesitation, and common-metrics papers

This is where the current prototype is most likely to over-read the literature. The crowd-navigation and common-metrics papers justify including `task_success_rate`, `task_completion_time`, `comfort_aware_path_efficiency`, and `hesitation`, but they do **not** provide the same kind of direct perception evidence as the proxemics and signaling studies. The freezing-robot papers are primarily about navigation failure modes and cooperation models; the common-metrics paper is a framework paper; the broader surveys and evaluation papers are about benchmarking principles rather than human judgments attached to specific cell magnitudes. citeturn7search5turn21view0turn15view1turn22search2turn27view0turn4search3turn23view1turn23view2

Recent benchmarking literature makes this caution sharper, not weaker. The 2022 evaluation review says the field still lacks agreed-upon evaluation protocols. The 2024 expert-interview paper reports that collision avoidance is the only near-universal priority, with other preferences varying by domain. The 2025 methodological benchmarking review explicitly notes that weighted averages are often arbitrary and that one metric cannot capture the complexity of social navigation. The 2023/2022 human-centered benchmarking papers show that efficiency-type ratios can correlate with human judgments, but only after empirical validation against human ratings rather than by fiat. citeturn27view0turn25view0turn26view0turn28view3turn29search1

The tuning implication is straightforward: `task_success_rate` can remain strong for **Dexterity**, but `task_completion_time`, `comfort_aware_path_efficiency`, and especially `hesitation` should stay weak-to-moderate until your own ratings say otherwise. Inference: these features are excellent candidates for **fitted** weights, not for strong **hand-authored** weights. citeturn25view0turn26view0turn29search1turn28view3

## What this means for tuning the current matrix

Relative to the current v0 matrix, the literature supports **keeping high priors** on the following relationships: `min_human_robot_distance → safety`, `proxemic_intrusion_dose → safety`, `proxemic_intrusion_dose → awareness`, `human_aware_approach → safety`, `human_aware_approach → awareness`, `gesture_response_success → awareness`, `acknowledgement_clarity → awareness`, `bystander_ack → awareness`, `legibility → awareness`, and `task_success_rate → dexterity`. Those are the cells for which there is the clearest convergence of direct studies, surveys, and newer benchmarking discussions. fileciteturn0file1L605-L760 citeturn0search10turn1search4turn1search16turn2search0turn3view2turn2search10turn2search7turn4search0turn16search0

The literature supports **downgrading or at least flagging more aggressively** the following cells: `comfort_aware_path_efficiency → awareness`, `hesitation → awareness`, `stability → awareness`, `behavioral_naturalness → awareness`, `sparc → dexterity`, `sparc → impression`, `heading_jerk → safety`, and the weak impression spillovers from distance and speed. Some of these cells are already marked as low-evidence in the spec, and that is correct; the audit simply extends the same caution to a few additional kinematic cells. This is an inference from the evidence tiers, not a claim that those relationships are false. fileciteturn0file1L645-L749 citeturn5search1turn11search1turn7search5turn21view0turn27view0turn28view3

The current file also gives the **Safety** column the largest total mass. That direction is defensible. Recent expert interviews found collisions to be the only near-universal priority across domains, and modern evaluation papers repeatedly treat safety as the first requirement before domain-specific tradeoffs are considered. A defensible inference for the eventual artifact is therefore: **use safety as a gate or lexicographic first stage, then let the remaining weights express domain style**. That is better aligned with the literature than allowing a flat weighted sum to buy back unsafe behavior with efficiency or naturalness points. fileciteturn0file1L753-L770 citeturn25view0turn26view0turn4search3turn23view1

A second tuning rule is to separate **direct perceptual evidence** from **survey/framework support**. Survey and review papers such as Ríos-Martínez, Kruse, Gao and Huang, Francis, Karwowski, and the 2025 benchmarking review should shape the **matrix structure** and the **choice of feature families**, but they should almost never be the sole reason a cell gets a top score of **3**. Top scores should usually require either repeated direct HRI rating evidence or very strong convergence from multiple direct studies. citeturn14search0turn14search5turn27view0turn4search3turn29search3turn23view1

A third rule is to avoid double counting. In the current spec, S12 and S29 are the same study, and several framework papers are cited across multiple sign rationales. For the tuned artifact, count **study families**, not just source IDs. Otherwise a single passing-distance paper and a single survey can accidentally dominate the prior by repetition. citeturn2search0turn2search8turn23view1

A fourth rule is to fix the **Awareness target variable** before fitting weights. RoSAS Competence, RoSAS Warmth, Godspeed Safety, and Godspeed Likeability are straightforward validated subscales. “PSI Awareness,” as currently named, is not. The safest literature-aligned move is to define a composite explicitly from named PSI scales and then treat that composite as a new reported measure, rather than claiming a nonexistent validated five-item PSI Awareness subscale. A reasonable literature-grounded direction would be a composite built from PSI scales related to identifying humans and recognizing/adapting to human behavior, but that would be your paper’s operationalization, not a direct citation-level fact. fileciteturn0file1L807-L813 citeturn17view1turn18view2

## Open questions and limitations

The main unresolved issue is **instrument validity on the Awareness axis**, not bibliography. Until the PSI target is specified precisely, any learned weights on that axis will be harder to interpret as “current literature aligned.” citeturn17view1turn18view2

A second unresolved issue is whether the final artifact should encode some features as **hard constraints or gates** rather than ordinary weighted terms. The recent literature strongly points in that direction, but the exact implementation belongs to the design stage after this summary. citeturn25view0turn26view0turn28view3

A third limitation is that some older conference items are easiest to verify through repository mirrors, institutional pages, or publisher snippets rather than clean modern landing pages. That affects citation neatness, not existence: the papers and author records are clearly real, but a few metadata fields are easier to verify from secondary repository mirrors than from publisher abstracts alone. citeturn22search1turn21view0turn0search10turn1search4

The highest-confidence conclusion remains simple: **retain strong priors for proxemics, approach geometry, and explicit intent communication; treat kinematic smoothness and efficiency features as low-to-medium priors; deduplicate repeated evidence; and correct the Awareness instrument before learning or defending the final weight matrix.** citeturn27view0turn25view0turn26view0turn23view1turn23view2
