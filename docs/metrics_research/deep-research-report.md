# Perceptual Aggregation Evidence Set for Social Navigation

## Scope and evidence model

This pass follows the uploaded plan’s requirement to build a **perceptual aggregation evidence set** while keeping **scale-validation evidence separate from behavior-to-perception evidence**. In the registry below, I therefore distinguish **scale_construct** sources from **condition_level** studies, **direct_dataset** resources, and broader **literature_prior** sources. The resulting priors are strongest for **proxemics, speed, legibility, and intent communication**, and materially weaker for **task success, completion time, path efficiency, and morphology-task fit**, where the literature retrieved here is more construct-definitional than trainable. fileciteturn0file0 citeturn18search9turn35view0

## Construct mapping

Below, each source is mapped only where the retrieved evidence supports a reasonable axis mapping. “Low” means the source is useful as a prior or conceptual anchor, but not a strong training signal.

- **S01 Godspeed** — original constructs: **Perceived Intelligence**, **Perceived Safety**, **Likeability**, **Animacy**, **Anthropomorphism**; scale/items used: standard Godspeed semantic-differential subscales; mapped axes: **perceived_dexterity** ← Perceived Intelligence (**high**), **perceived_safety** ← Perceived Safety (**high**), **impression** ← Likeability/Animacy/Anthropomorphism (**high/medium**); rationale: these subscales are almost direct semantic matches to dexterity, safety, and global impression. citeturn0search0

- **S02 RoSAS** — original constructs: **Competence**, **Warmth**, **Discomfort**; scale/items used: RoSAS Likert items; mapped axes: **perceived_dexterity** ← Competence (**high**), **perceived_safety** ← inverse Discomfort (**high**), **impression** ← Warmth and inverse Discomfort (**high**), **perceived_social_awareness** ← Warmth (**medium**); rationale: competence is the cleanest dexterity proxy, while discomfort captures perceived threat/unease and warmth partly captures socially aware demeanor. citeturn0search1turn1search3

- **S03 NARS** — original construct: **negative attitudes toward robots**; scale/items used: NARS subscales; mapped axes: **perceived_safety** (**medium**), **impression** (**medium**); rationale: NARS primarily measures prior anxiety/negativity, not task-level perception, so it is best treated as a moderating prior rather than a direct label. citeturn3search9turn3search11

- **S04 Almere** — original constructs: acceptance, trust, anxiety, sociability, social presence, enjoyment, usefulness; scale/items used: Almere acceptance model questionnaire; mapped axes: **perceived_safety** ← Trust/Anxiety (**high**), **perceived_social_awareness** ← Sociability/Social Presence (**high**), **impression** ← Enjoyment/Attitude/Acceptance (**medium/high**), **perceived_dexterity** ← Perceived Usefulness/Ease of Use (**low/medium**); rationale: Almere is acceptance-oriented, so it is useful for latent axis semantics but not for direct navigation labels. citeturn0search2turn2view2

- **S05 GAToRS** — original construct: **general attitudes toward robots**; scale/items used: GAToRS questionnaire; mapped axes: **impression** (**medium**), **perceived_safety** (**low/medium**); rationale: broad attitudinal stance shapes robot valence and risk sensitivity, but is not task-conditional. citeturn0search3turn2view5

- **S06 Dautenhahn 2006** — original constructs: preferred approach direction, comfort, stopping distance/practicality; scale/items used: questionnaires after live trials; mapped axes: **perceived_safety** (**high**), **perceived_social_awareness** (**high**), **impression** (**medium**); rationale: avoidance of frontal approach and preference for side approach indicate that socially appropriate approach geometry is read primarily as safety/social awareness. citeturn27view0turn28view0

- **S07 Woods 2006** — original constructs: comfort, efficiency, preferred/least preferred approach direction; scale/items used: 5-point Likert comfort/efficiency ratings and categorical preferences; mapped axes: **perceived_safety** (**high**), **perceived_social_awareness** (**medium/high**), **impression** (**medium**); rationale: comfort and preferred approach direction operationalize whether the robot is reading the social situation appropriately. citeturn24view0turn25view0

- **S08 Pacchierotti 2005** — original constructs: comfort with overall behavior, speed, signaling distance, lateral distance; scale/items used: 1–5 comfort ratings; mapped axes: **perceived_safety** (**high**), **perceived_social_awareness** (**high**), **impression** (**medium**); rationale: early signaling was described as making behavior trustworthy and clearly understood, while speed and spacing shaped comfort. citeturn10view0turn11view0

## Feature-axis evidence matrix

Only relations with at least some supporting evidence are listed. When the evidence is construct-level rather than behavior-to-perception, I label it accordingly and keep confidence conservative.

- **S01/S02 — objective feature: `task_success_rate`; subjective axis: `perceived_dexterity`; expected sign: positive; evidence level: literature_prior / scale_construct; statistic: not estimated in surfaced sources; key finding: competence/perceived-intelligence constructs are the canonical latent targets for “the robot can do the task well”; confidence: low/medium; limitations: this pass did not surface a primary navigation study directly regressing success rate on competence ratings. citeturn0search0turn0search1

- **S19 — objective feature: `task_completion_time`; subjective axis: `perceived_dexterity`; expected sign: negative; evidence level: literature_prior; statistic: not estimated; key finding: social robot navigation evaluation frameworks treat efficiency as important but subordinate to comfort, safety, and legibility, implying slower-than-necessary behavior can lower perceived competence; confidence: low; limitations: no direct trainable HRI result was surfaced in this pass. citeturn18search9

- **S19/S12 — objective feature: `path_efficiency`; subjective axis: `perceived_dexterity`; expected sign: positive; evidence level: literature_prior + condition_level; statistic: not estimated; key finding: navigation should remain efficient, but Neggers shows comfort saturates with larger passing distances, implying that a purely shortest-path policy is not the perceptually optimal one; confidence: low/medium; limitations: the retrieved literature is richer on comfort trade-offs than on explicit “efficiency → competence” coefficients. citeturn18search9turn9view0

## Gaps and risks

- **Missing evidence for direct trainable links from classic performance metrics to perception.** The retrieved set is rich for proxemics and intent communication, but relatively poor for direct regressions from `task_success_rate`, `task_completion_time`, and `path_efficiency` to subjective axes. Those cells should therefore start as weak priors and be updated only once matched datasets are assembled. citeturn18search9turn35view0

- **Weak mappings for `morphology_task_fit`.** The scale literature clearly supports that embodiment and general attitudes affect overall impression, but this pass did not surface a strong primary mobile-navigation study directly linking morphology-task fit to the four axes. Use **expert prior** here until a better evidence base is collected. citeturn0search0turn0search1turn0search3

- **SPARC and smoothness are valuable but easy to overclaim.** S18 validates smoothness as a motion property, not as an HRI perception label. S17 is useful because it pairs a velocity-profile manipulation with Godspeed, but the effects were mostly not statistically significant at the full-scale level. Smoothness should therefore be treated as a **mechanistic feature prior**, not a proven perceptual driver on its own. citeturn31view3turn35view0

- **Several important sources are scales only, not trainable data.** S01–S05 are essential for defining latent targets and questionnaire content, but they should not be confused with behavioral supervision. They help specify what “dexterity,” “safety,” “social awareness,” and “impression” ought to mean, yet they do not provide navigation-feature labels by themselves. citeturn0search0turn0search1turn3search9turn0search2turn0search3

- **Condition-level studies are valuable but heterogeneous.** The dominant retrieved experiments vary in robot morphology, corridor width, seated vs moving humans, teleoperation vs autonomy, and whether the task is passing, helping, or collaboration. That heterogeneity is useful for priors, but it raises transportability risk if one tries to pool them as if they were a single benchmark. citeturn27view0turn24view0turn11view0turn9view0turn7view6

- **Public raw datasets remain scarce.** The clearest directly reusable public resource surfaced here is **Vid2Real**. Most other items are open papers with condition means or qualitative summaries, not downloadable participant-level navigation-plus-perception tables. This means the recommended next dataset-assembly step should probably combine **manual extraction**, **video re-annotation**, and **new data collection** rather than relying on a single canonical repository. citeturn36search8turn36search20turn18search9

- **Where expert prior is appropriate until validated.** The strongest candidates for expert-prior treatment are `task_completion_time → perceived_dexterity`, `path_efficiency → perceived_dexterity`, `morphology_task_fit → impression`, and `stability_controlledness → impression/social_awareness`. These links are plausible and likely directionally correct, but the surfaced literature here does not yet make them high-confidence trainable claims. citeturn18search9turn35view0
